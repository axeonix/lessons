# Queen Esther: For Such a Time as This (Esther 4:14)

**Opening Prayer**

## Introduction

**Esther 4:14**
> “And who knows but that you have come to your royal position for such a time as this?” (NIV)

### God Put You Here for a Reason

- Have you ever looked at your life and wondered: “Why am I here?” Why was I born into this family?
- Why did God allow me to go through that situation? Why am I working at this particular place?
- Why did God put these people in my life? Why am I facing this particular battle at this particular time?
- Sometimes we look at our circumstances and think everything is random. But the story of Esther shows us something powerful.
- God can take an **ordinary person**, place them in an **extraordinary position**, and use them for an **extraordinary purpose**.

### Who Was Esther?

- Esther was not born a queen. She was an orphan.
- She was raised by her cousin Mordecai. She was a Jewish woman living in a foreign empire.
- Yet God was positioning her for something she could not see coming.
- She did not understand why she had been chosen. She did not understand why she was in the palace.
- She did not know that her people were about to face destruction.
- **But God knew.**
- You may not understand where God has placed you. But God knows exactly where you are.
- You may not understand your circumstances, the timing, or the struggle. But God sees the whole picture when we can only see one piece.

---

## Point 1: God Can Position You Before You Understand Your Purpose

**Esther 2:17**
> “Now the king was attracted to Esther more than to any of the other women, and she won his favor and approval more than any of the other virgins. So he set a royal crown on her head and made her queen instead of Vashti.”

- Think about this. Esther did not wake up one morning and say: “Today I will become queen so I can save my people.”
- She did not know what was coming. **God was positioning her before she understood her purpose.**
- Sometimes God does the same thing with us:
  * He may open a door that does not make sense.
  * He may move you into a position you did not expect.
  * He may introduce you to someone you did not know you needed to meet.
  * He may allow you to go through something you do not understand.
- Years later, you may look back and say: “God was preparing me for something.”

**Romans 8:28**
> “And we know that in all things God works for the good of those who love him, who have been called according to his purpose.”

- Notice what this verse does **not** say. It does not say that everything that happens is good.
- It says: **“In all things God works for the good.”** There is a difference.
  * The betrayal was not good.
  * The disappointment was not good.
  * The sickness was not good.
  * The loss was not good.
  * The difficult season was not good.
- But God can take things that were never good and work them together for His purposes.
- Esther could not see the purpose when she entered the palace. But God could.
- Perhaps you are in a season right now where you cannot see what God is doing.
- Do not assume that because you cannot see God’s hand, God is not working.
- God can be working behind the scenes while you are wondering what is happening.

---

## Point 2: When God Calls You to Stand, Do Not Let Fear Make the Decision

### The Crisis

- Mordecai discovered that Haman had devised a plan to destroy the Jewish people.
- Haman convinced King Xerxes to issue a decree for the Jews to be killed (**Esther 3:13**).
- Esther was now in a position to do something about it. But there was a serious problem.
- She could not simply walk into the king’s presence.
- Anyone who approached the king without being summoned could be put to death (**Esther 4:11**).
- Esther was afraid. And honestly, she had every reason to be afraid.

### Mordecai’s Message

**Esther 4:13-14**
> “Do not think that because you are in the king’s house you alone of all the Jews will escape. For if you remain silent at this time, relief and deliverance for the Jews will arise from another place, but you and your father’s family will perish. And who knows but that you have come to your royal position for such a time as this?”

- That sentence should get our attention: **“For such a time as this.”**
- Mordecai was saying: Esther, maybe this is why you are here.
- Maybe God did not put you in the palace just so you could wear a crown. Maybe God put you there because somebody needed you.
- Maybe your position was not about **privilege**. Maybe it was about **responsibility**.
- And that applies to us.
- God does not bless us just so we can consume His blessings. He blesses us so we can **become a blessing** to others.
- God gives us influence so we can influence people toward Him.
- God gives us opportunities so we can use them for His glory.
- God gives us a testimony so we can tell somebody else what He has done.

### Esther’s Answer

**Esther 4:16**
> “…When this is done, I will go to the king, even though it is against the law. And if I perish, I perish.”

- That is courage. Notice she did not say: “I am not afraid.”
- She said: **“If I perish, I perish.”** She was afraid — but she refused to let fear control her obedience.
- Sometimes we think courage means you are not afraid. It does not.
- **Courage means fear does not get the final word.**
- There may be something God is asking you to do that scares you:
  * Maybe you need to forgive someone.
  * Maybe you need to apologize.
  * Maybe you need to walk away from something you know is wrong.
  * Maybe you need to tell somebody about Jesus.
  * Maybe you need to take a stand for your faith.
  * Maybe you need to stop living halfway between the world and God.
- Whatever it is: Do not let fear make a decision that God is calling you to make by faith.

---

## Point 3: Before Esther Fought the Battle, She Sought God

- This may be one of the most important lessons in the entire story.
- Esther did not immediately run into the king’s presence. **She called for a fast.**

**Esther 4:16**
> “Go, gather together all the Jews who are in Susa, and fast for me. Do not eat or drink for three days, night or day. I and my attendants will fast as you do.”

- Esther understood something: this battle was bigger than her. She needed God’s help.
- Before she confronted the king, she prepared herself spiritually.
- There is a lesson here for us.
- We live in a world that teaches us to solve everything ourselves:
  * “Work harder.”
  * “Be stronger.”
  * “Believe in yourself.”
- But the Christian life begins with something different: **“Trust in God.”**

- Esther understood that before she approached the king, she needed to seek God.

### God Turned It Around

- After the fasting, Esther went to the king. God gave her favor — the king held out the gold scepter to her (**Esther 5:2**).
- The king listened. Haman’s plot was exposed (**Esther 7**).
- The very man who planned to destroy God’s people faced judgment himself.
- The situation was completely turned around (**Esther 9:1**).
- What looked like defeat became victory. What looked like the end became a new beginning.
- What looked like a hidden God was actually a God who had been working the entire time.

---

## Point 4: God’s Name May Not Be Seen, but His Hand Is There

- There is something fascinating about the book of Esther. The name of God does not appear in the book.
- Yet God’s fingerprints are everywhere:
  * Esther becomes queen (**Esther 2:17**).
  * Mordecai discovers a plot against the king (**Esther 2:21-23**).
  * The king cannot sleep (**Esther 6:1**).
  * The king happens to remember what Mordecai did (**Esther 6:2-3**).
  * Haman happens to be in the right place at the wrong time (**Esther 6:4-6**).
  * The entire situation reverses.
- Coincidence? No. **Providence.**
- God does not always announce what He is doing:
  * Sometimes He works quietly.
  * Sometimes He works through circumstances.
  * Sometimes He works through people.
  * Sometimes He works through an open door.
  * Sometimes He works through a closed door.
- Sometimes we do not recognize what God is doing until we look backward. That is why we need faith.
- Faith does not require us to see the entire road. Faith simply requires us to trust the One who does.

**2 Corinthians 5:7**
> “For we live by faith, not by sight.”

- You do not have to see everything to trust God. You do not have to understand everything to obey God.
- You do not have to know the outcome before you take the next step. You just need to know **who** is leading you.

---

## Point 5: What Is Your “For Such a Time as This”?

- Esther’s story is not just about an ancient queen. It is about God using ordinary people at extraordinary moments.
  * Maybe you are a parent. Your children are watching you.
  * Maybe you are a grandparent. Your grandchildren are watching you.
  * Maybe you are a husband or wife. Your spouse is watching your faith.
  * Maybe you are a coworker. Someone near you may need to see what genuine Christianity looks like.
  * Maybe you are a friend. Someone may be one conversation away from hearing about Jesus.
- Maybe you are facing a difficult situation right now. Perhaps God put you in that situation because He wants to use your faithfulness as a testimony.
- You may be thinking: “I am nobody special.” Neither was Esther.
- You may be thinking: “I do not have much.” Esther did not start with much.
- You may be thinking: “I am afraid.” Esther was afraid too.
- You may be thinking: “I do not know what to do.” Esther did not know what was going to happen.
- But she trusted God and stepped forward. And that is what God may be asking of you.
- Not perfection. Not greatness. Not fame.
- **Just faithfulness.**

---

## Point 6: The Greatest Deliverance

- There is something even greater than the deliverance of the Jewish people in Esther’s story.
- There is another enemy that every human being needs deliverance from. **Sin.**
- Our greatest problem is not financial. It is not political, physical, or social.
- Our greatest problem is that we have sinned against a holy God.

**Romans 3:23**
> “For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.”

- Every one of us needs a Savior. And that is where Jesus Christ comes in.
- Esther **risked** her life to approach an earthly king on behalf of her people.
- But Jesus did not merely risk His life. Jesus **gave** His life.

**Romans 5:8**
> “But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us.”

- Jesus went to the cross because we could not save ourselves. He took the punishment that we deserved.
- He died. He was buried. And He rose again.
- Because Jesus rose from the grave, we can have forgiveness, salvation, and eternal life.

### The Throne Above Every Throne

- Do you see the difference between King Xerxes’ throne and the Throne of God?
- A throne is the seat of an exalted ruler. The one who sits on the throne has the power to make anything happen in his kingdom.
- Anyone who stands before that throne must stand with the utmost respect.

**Esther 4:11**
> “All the king’s officials and the people of the royal provinces know that for any man or woman who approaches the king in the inner court without being summoned the king has but one law: that they be put to death unless the king extends the gold scepter to them and spares their lives. But thirty days have passed since I was called to go to the king.”

- Esther was the king’s own wife. Yet the most dangerous place she could go was into her husband’s throne room without permission.
- To enter this king’s presence uninvited was to invite death.
- Mordecai urged her throughout chapter 4 to act for the Jews. She went anyway.
- By risking her own life, she opened the way for thousands of her people to be saved.

**Hebrews 4:16**
> “Let us then approach God’s throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us in our time of need.”

- Now see how differently God does it. Esther had to hope the king would hold out the scepter.
- God’s chosen ones are **invited** to come with confidence. This is the great privilege God gives to us.
- And the One on this throne is far greater than Xerxes.

---

## Conclusion: For Such a Time as This

- Esther did not know how the story would end. But she trusted God.
- And when her moment came, she stepped forward. Maybe your moment is here.
- Do not let fear control you. Do not remain silent when God has called you to speak.
- **Pray. Trust. Stand firm. Obey.**
- You do not have to know everything God is doing. You simply have to trust the God who does.
- Because just as God used Esther, God can use you.

**Perhaps the reason God has brought you to this very moment is because you have been placed here for such a time as this.**
> *“And who knows but that you have come to your royal position for such a time as this?”* — Esther 4:14

God Bless you all in The Mighty Name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth.
