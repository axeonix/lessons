# Do Not Wait Until Midnight: The Parable of the Ten Virgins (Matthew 25:1-13)

**Opening Prayer**

## Introduction

**Matthew 25:1-13**
> ***The Parable of the Ten Virgins***
>
> <sup>1</sup> “At that time the kingdom of heaven will be like ten virgins who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom. <sup>2</sup> Five of them were foolish and five were wise. <sup>3</sup> The foolish ones took their lamps but did not take any oil with them. <sup>4</sup> The wise ones, however, took oil in jars along with their lamps. <sup>5</sup> The bridegroom was a long time in coming, and they all became drowsy and fell asleep.
>
> <sup>6</sup> “At midnight the cry rang out: ‘Here’s the bridegroom! Come out to meet him!’
>
> <sup>7</sup> “Then all the virgins woke up and trimmed their lamps. <sup>8</sup> The foolish ones said to the wise, ‘Give us some of your oil; our lamps are going out.’
>
> <sup>9</sup> “‘No,’ they replied, ‘there may not be enough for both us and you. Instead, go to those who sell oil and buy some for yourselves.’
>
> <sup>10</sup> “But while they were on their way to buy the oil, the bridegroom arrived. The virgins who were ready went in with him to the wedding banquet. And the door was shut.
>
> <sup>11</sup> “Later the others also came. ‘Lord, Lord,’ they said, ‘open the door for us!’
>
> <sup>12</sup> “But he replied, ‘Truly I tell you, I don’t know you.’
>
> <sup>13</sup> “Therefore keep watch, because you do not know the day or the hour.”

- If Jesus Christ returned today, would you be ready to meet Him?
- Not tomorrow, not next week, and not after you have finally decided to take your relationship with God seriously.
- In this parable, Jesus tells the story of ten virgins waiting for a bridegroom. Five were wise, and five were foolish.
- All ten had lamps. All ten were waiting. All ten expected the bridegroom to arrive.
- Yet when he came, only five were prepared to enter the wedding banquet.
- The others discovered, too late, that they had not prepared properly.
- Jesus concludes with this warning: **“Therefore keep watch, because you do not know the day or the hour”** (**Matthew 25:13**).
- This is not merely a story about an ancient wedding.
- It is a warning about spiritual readiness, the return of Christ, and the importance of knowing Him personally.

---

## Point 1: All Ten Were Waiting, but Not All Ten Were Ready

**Matthew 25:1-4**

- The ten virgins had much in common.
- They were waiting for the same bridegroom. They carried lamps, and they expected to take part in the wedding celebration.
- But there was one critical difference. The wise virgins brought extra oil. The foolish virgins did not.
- This is where the parable speaks directly to the church today.
- People can attend church every Sunday, know the Scriptures, and sing worship songs.
- They can take part in Christian activities without having a genuine relationship with Jesus.
- The question is not simply: “Do you have a lamp?” The question is: **“Are you prepared for the Bridegroom?”**
- Having a Bible does not automatically mean we obey God’s Word.
- **Knowing about Jesus is not the same as following Him.**

**2 Corinthians 13:5**
> “Examine yourselves to see whether you are in the faith; test yourselves. Do you not realize that Christ Jesus is in you—unless, of course, you fail the test?”

- This verse teaches us to examine ourselves to see whether we are in the faith. Ask yourself:
  * Am I following Jesus when nobody is watching?
  * Am I willing to repent when I am wrong?
  * Is my faith growing, or am I simply going through the motions?
  * Do I know Jesus personally, or do I only know about Him?
- The ten virgins remind us that outward appearances cannot replace genuine spiritual readiness.

---

## Point 2: What Does the Oil Represent?

**Matthew 25:3-4**

- The oil is central to this parable. All ten virgins had lamps, but only five brought an extra supply.
- Jesus does not say exactly what the oil represents. So we must be careful not to claim more than the passage states.
- But the oil gives us a powerful picture of the spiritual preparation we need to stay ready for Christ.

### The Holy Spirit

**Galatians 5:16**
> “So I say, walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh.”

- The Holy Spirit convicts us, guides us, and strengthens us. He helps us live in obedience to God.
- The oil can remind us of our dependence on the Holy Spirit.

### A Genuine Relationship with Jesus

**John 15:4**
> “Remain in me, as I also remain in you. No branch can bear fruit by itself; it must remain in the vine. Neither can you bear fruit unless you remain in me.”

- Jesus teaches that we must remain in Him to bear fruit.
- Christianity is not just knowing facts about Christ.
- It is trusting Him, following Him, and remaining in fellowship with Him.

### Faith That Perseveres

**Hebrews 10:36**
> “You need to persevere so that when you have done the will of God, you will receive what he has promised.”

- The bridegroom took a long time to arrive. The five wise virgins had prepared for the delay.
- Genuine faith must endure through difficulties, unanswered prayers, and temptation.
- It must endure through seasons when God seems distant.

### Prayer, Repentance, and Obedience

- A healthy spiritual life includes prayer, Scripture, worship, fellowship, repentance, and obedience.
- These practices do not purchase salvation.
- **Ephesians 2:8-10** teaches that salvation is God’s gift of grace, received through faith.
- And it teaches that we are created in Christ Jesus to do good works.
- The oil reminds us that genuine spiritual readiness cannot be replaced by religious appearances.
- We can encourage one another, but nobody can believe in our place.
- Our parents’ faith, our pastor’s prayers, and our church attendance cannot replace our own personal response to Jesus.

---

## Point 3: The Delay Revealed Who Had Prepared

**Matthew 25:5-7**

- Jesus tells us the bridegroom was delayed, and all ten virgins became drowsy and fell asleep.
- Notice something important: **all ten fell asleep**, even the wise virgins.
- The difference was not that the wise never became tired. It was that they had prepared for the delay.
- At midnight, a cry rang out: “Here’s the bridegroom! Come out to meet him!”
- The virgins woke up and prepared their lamps.
- The wise were ready. But the foolish discovered that their oil was running out.
- The lesson is clear: **preparation must happen before the moment arrives.**
  * You do not wait until the storm begins to build a foundation.
  * You do not wait until an emergency to decide whether prayer matters.
  * And you should not wait until you believe Jesus is about to return to take your relationship with Him seriously.
- Some people say: “I will get serious about God later.”
  * Later, when I am older.
  * Later, when life gets easier.
  * Later, when I have more time.
- But we are not promised tomorrow.
- **James 4:14** reminds us that our lives are like a mist that appears for a little while and then vanishes.
- **2 Peter 3:9** reminds us of God’s patience and His desire for people to come to repentance.
- But His patience should never be mistaken for a promise that we can put off our response forever.
- **The time to prepare is now.**

---

## Point 4: You Cannot Borrow Someone Else’s Oil

**Matthew 25:8-9**

- When the foolish virgins saw that their lamps were going out, they asked the wise virgins to share their oil.
- But the wise virgins explained that there might not be enough for everyone.
- The point is not that Christians should refuse to help others. Scripture calls us to love, serve, and encourage one another.
- The point is that personal spiritual readiness cannot simply be transferred from one person to another.
  * Your pastor can preach the truth, but he cannot believe in your place.
  * Your spouse can pray for you, but cannot surrender your heart to Christ for you.
  * Your parents can teach you the Bible, but they cannot make your decision to follow Jesus.
- **Romans 14:12** teaches that each of us will give an account of ourselves to God.
- We should pray for others, encourage struggling believers, and help people grow.
- But nobody can substitute their relationship with Jesus for ours.
- You cannot wait until the final moment to begin taking God seriously.
- This does not mean a person cannot sincerely repent late in life.
- God’s grace can reach people even at the end of life, as we see with the thief on the cross.
- The warning is against assuming that another opportunity is guaranteed.
- **Today is the time to trust Christ, repent, and follow Him.**

---

## Point 5: The Cost of Discipleship

- The wise virgins told the foolish virgins to go and buy oil. What does this mean?
- We must be careful. Salvation and the Holy Spirit cannot be purchased through prayer, fasting, Bible reading, or religious effort.
- **Ephesians 2:8-9** teaches that we are saved by grace through faith, not by our own works.
- We cannot buy forgiveness or earn eternal life.
- But there is a cost to discipleship. It is not the purchase price of salvation. It is the cost of surrendering our lives to Jesus.
  * It costs us pride when we admit we are wrong.
  * It costs us comfort when God calls us to obey.
  * It costs us selfishness when we put others first.
  * It costs us cherished sins when we repent.
- In **Luke 9:23**, Jesus teaches that anyone who wants to follow Him must deny themselves, take up their cross daily, and follow Him.
- We do not do these things to earn God’s favor.
- We do them because we have received His grace, and we want to follow Him faithfully.
- **Grace is free, but following Jesus means surrendering ownership of your life to Him.**
- The wise virgins remind us to take personal responsibility for our spiritual preparation.
- We must not assume someone else will solve the problem when the bridegroom arrives.

---

## Point 6: The Warning of the Laodicean Church

**Revelation 3:14-22**
> ***To the Church in Laodicea***
>
> <sup>14</sup> “To the angel of the church in Laodicea write: These are the words of the Amen, the faithful and true witness, the ruler of God’s creation. <sup>15</sup> I know your deeds, that you are neither cold nor hot. I wish you were either one or the other! <sup>16</sup> So, because you are lukewarm—neither hot nor cold—I am about to spit you out of my mouth. <sup>17</sup> You say, ‘I am rich; I have acquired wealth and do not need a thing.’ But you do not realize that you are wretched, pitiful, poor, blind and naked. <sup>18</sup> I counsel you to buy from me gold refined in the fire, so you can become rich; and white clothes to wear, so you can cover your shameful nakedness; and salve to put on your eyes, so you can see. <sup>19</sup> Those whom I love I rebuke and discipline. So be earnest and repent. <sup>20</sup> Here I am! I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears my voice and opens the door, I will come in and eat with that person, and they with me.
>
> <sup>21</sup> To the one who is victorious, I will give the right to sit with me on my throne, just as I was victorious and sat down with my Father on his throne. <sup>22</sup> Whoever has ears, let them hear what the Spirit says to the churches.”

- Jesus spoke to the church in Laodicea, a church that thought it was wealthy and needed nothing.
- But in verse 17, Jesus exposes its true spiritual condition.
- The church believed it was doing well. Yet Jesus said it did not see its own spiritual poverty.
- This is one of the greatest dangers of complacency.
- We can become so comfortable with religious routines that we stop examining our hearts.
- We may believe we are spiritually rich because we know the Bible, attend church, or have been Christians for many years.
- But what does Jesus see?
  * Are we growing in love?
  * Are we living in obedience?
  * Are we becoming more like Christ?
  * Are we humble enough to receive correction?
- In verse 18, Jesus counsels the church to receive from Him what it truly needs.
- He uses the pictures of gold refined by fire, white clothes, and salve for the eyes.
- These represent our need for genuine spiritual riches, righteousness, and restored spiritual sight.

### Laodicea Compared with Smyrna

**Revelation 2:9**
> “I know your afflictions and your poverty—yet you are rich! I know about the slander of those who say they are Jews and are not, but are a synagogue of Satan.”

- Smyrna looked poor in worldly terms, but Jesus saw its spiritual wealth.
- Laodicea looked rich, but Jesus exposed its spiritual poverty.
- Whose evaluation matters most, ours or His?
- We may impress other people and even deceive ourselves. But we cannot hide our hearts from God.
- It does not matter how much money you have.
- If you do not have the Word of God, you are living in spiritual poverty. That is the worst kind of poverty anyone could have.
- Jesus calls the Laodicean church to see its condition and repent.
- His warning reminds us never to become so comfortable that we stop listening to Him.

---

## Point 7: When the Door Was Shut

**Matthew 25:10-12**
> “But while they were on their way to buy the oil, the bridegroom arrived. The virgins who were ready went in with him to the wedding banquet. And the door was shut. <sup>11</sup> Later the others also came. ‘Lord, Lord,’ they said, ‘open the door for us!’ <sup>12</sup> But he replied, ‘Truly I tell you, I don’t know you.’”

- This is the most sobering moment in the parable.
- While the foolish virgins were away trying to get oil, the bridegroom arrived.
- The wise virgins entered the wedding banquet, and **the door was shut.**
- When the foolish virgins returned, they pleaded for the door to be opened.
- But the bridegroom replied: **“I don’t know you.”**
- The foolish virgins wanted to enter, but they had failed to prepare.
- The closed door shows how serious and final that moment is.
- Jesus warns us not to assume that an opportunity we have today will always be there tomorrow.
- In **Matthew 7:21-23**, Jesus gives a similar warning. He speaks of people who claim religious accomplishments but do not truly belong to Him.
- Christians understand the foolish virgins in different ways.
  * Some see them as people who were part of the visible church but never had genuine saving faith.
  * Others emphasize the warning to believers to persevere and stay prepared.
- The passage does not tell us the foolish virgins’ spiritual history. So we should not claim more certainty than Jesus gives us.
- What we can say is this: they were not prepared when the bridegroom arrived, and their failure had serious consequences.
- This parable is not an invitation to guess about everyone else’s salvation. **It is a call to examine our own hearts.**
- Our hope is not in our own perfection. It is in Jesus Christ, who saves by grace and calls us to follow Him faithfully.

---

## Point 8: The Midnight Cry: Jesus Is Coming

**Matthew 25:6, 13**

- The bridegroom arrived unexpectedly.
- Jesus teaches that we do not know the day or hour of His return.
- We should not try to calculate the date or predict the exact moment. Instead, we are called to live faithfully today.
- People go to work, raise families, make plans, and pursue their goals. These ordinary activities are not wrong.
- The danger comes when we become so absorbed in this life that we forget eternity.
- **Romans 13:11** urges believers to wake up from spiritual sleep.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:6** calls Christians to stay alert and self-controlled.
- Being watchful does not mean living in panic.
- It means remembering that our lives belong to God, and that Christ will return as He promised.
- Ask yourself:
  * Would Jesus find me living in faith?
  * Would He find me loving and forgiving others?
  * Would He find me pursuing holiness?
  * Would He find me sharing the Gospel?
  * Would He find me trusting Him through difficult times?
- **The best way to prepare for the return of Jesus is to follow Jesus faithfully today.**

---

## Point 9: How Do We Keep Our Lamps Burning?

- **Remain in God’s Word.**
  * Read Scripture to know God, understand His will, and let His truth shape your life.
  * **Psalm 119:105** describes God’s Word as a lamp for our feet and a light for our path.
- **Keep a life of prayer.**
  * Prayer is a time to worship, confess, give thanks, seek wisdom, and give our concerns to God.
  * **1 Thessalonians 5:17** calls us to pray continually.
- **Repent and pursue holiness.**
  * Do not become comfortable with sin. When God shows you something that needs to change, respond humbly.
  * **1 Peter 1:15-16** calls believers to be holy in all they do.
- **Depend on the Holy Spirit.**
  * Ask God to strengthen you and shape your character.
  * **Galatians 5:22-23** describes the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.
- **Love others and share the Gospel.**
  * Forgive, serve, encourage, and tell others about Jesus.
  * We do not know how much time we, or the people around us, have.
- These practices do not purchase salvation.
- They are the expressions of a life that trusts Christ and seeks to follow Him faithfully.

---

## Conclusion: Do Not Wait Until Midnight

- The message of the ten virgins is simple, but it is urgent.
- The Bridegroom is coming. We do not know the day or the hour.
- We cannot depend on someone else’s faith.
- And we cannot replace a genuine relationship with Jesus with outward religious appearances.
- Do not assume you will always have more time.
- Keep your heart close to Jesus. Remain faithful in prayer. Stay grounded in God’s Word.
- Repent when you sin. Depend on the Holy Spirit.
- Love people as Christ has loved you, and share the Gospel while you have the opportunity.
- **Above all, put your faith in Jesus Christ.**
- The question is not whether we can predict His return. The question is whether we are living in a way that reflects our faith in Him.
- One day, the waiting will be over. The midnight cry will come. The Bridegroom will arrive.
- May we be found ready. Not because we trusted in our own goodness, but because we placed our faith in Jesus Christ and kept following Him.
- Do not wait to get ready. Do not settle for appearances. Do not take God’s grace for granted.

**Keep watch. Stay faithful. Be ready.**
> *“Therefore keep watch, because you do not know the day or the hour.”* — Matthew 25:13

God Bless you all in The Mighty Name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth.
