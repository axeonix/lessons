# If God Already Knows Your Future, Do You Really Have Free Will?

**Opening Prayer**

## Introduction: A Question That Challenges Our Understanding

- Have you ever wondered whether your life is already written before you live it?
- Before you woke up this morning, God already knew every thought you would have and every word you would speak.
- He knew every decision you would make and every person you would meet.
- God knows your past, present, and future. Nothing surprises Him, and nothing catches Him off guard.
- But this raises an important question: If God already knows every decision I will make, do I really have the freedom to choose differently?
- If God knows what I will do tomorrow, how can He hold me responsible for my choices?
- Christians have wrestled with these questions for centuries.
- We may not fully understand how God’s perfect foreknowledge and human responsibility fit together. But we must begin with what Scripture teaches.
- Here is the central question: **Does God knowing what you will do mean He forces you to do it?**
- Let us examine the Word of God.

---

## Point 1: God Knows Everything About You

**Psalm 139:1-4**
> <sup>1</sup> You have searched me, LORD,<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;and you know me.<br>
> <sup>2</sup> You know when I sit and when I rise;<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;you perceive my thoughts from afar.<br>
> <sup>3</sup> You discern my going out and my lying down;<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;you are familiar with all my ways.<br>
> <sup>4</sup> Before a word is on my tongue<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;you, LORD, know it completely.

- Think about what David is telling us.
- God knows your thoughts, struggles, motives, intentions, and words before you speak them.
- He knows you more completely than you know yourself.

**Isaiah 46:9-10**
> <sup>9</sup> Remember the former things, those of long ago;<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;I am God, and there is no other;<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;I am God, and there is none like me.<br>
> <sup>10</sup> I make known the end from the beginning,<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;from ancient times, what is still to come.<br>
> I say, ‘My purpose will stand,<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;and I will do all that I please.’

- God makes known **“the end from the beginning,”** and His purpose will stand.
- We make plans without knowing what tomorrow holds. God does not have our limited view. He knows what is still to come.
- We must see an important difference here: **knowing what someone will do is not the same as forcing that person to do it.**

### An Illustration

- Imagine watching a recording of a football game.
- You know which team will win and which player will score. But your knowledge does not mean you caused those events.
- Of course, God’s perfect foreknowledge is far greater than watching a recording.
- This picture simply helps us separate knowing from causing.
- We may not understand exactly how God’s foreknowledge relates to human freedom.
- But we should not assume that knowing every decision means God forces every decision.

---

## Point 2: If God Knows Our Choices, Why Does He Tell Us to Choose?

**Deuteronomy 30:19**
> “This day I call the heavens and the earth as witnesses against you that I have set before you life and death, blessings and curses. Now choose life, so that you and your children may live.”

- Moses gave Israel a choice: life or death, blessings or curses, faithfulness or disobedience.
- He did not tell them their decisions were meaningless because God already knew their future. **He called them to choose life.**

**Joshua 24:15**
> “But if serving the LORD seems undesirable to you, then choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve … But as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD.”

- Joshua challenged the people to decide whom they would serve.
- He did not tell them there was no reason to choose because God already knew their future.
- All through Scripture, God calls people to repent, believe, obey, turn away from sin, and follow Him.
- Nobody ends up in hell by accident, just as nobody ends up in Heaven by accident. **They made their choice.**

**Ezekiel 18:30**
> “Repent! Turn away from all your offenses; then sin will not be your downfall.”

- Why does God command people to repent? Because their response to Him matters.
- The Bible never gives us permission to use God’s foreknowledge as an excuse for disobedience.
- **God’s knowledge of your future does not remove your responsibility to obey Him today.**

---

## Point 3: God’s Sovereignty and Human Responsibility at the Cross

- One of the clearest examples of these two truths is the crucifixion of Jesus Christ.

**Acts 2:23**
> “This man was handed over to you by God’s deliberate plan and foreknowledge; and you, with the help of wicked men, put him to death by nailing him to the cross.”

- Notice the two truths in this verse.
- **First,** Jesus’ death was part of God’s deliberate plan and foreknowledge.
- The cross was not an accident. It was central to God’s plan of redemption.
- **Second,** Peter held the people responsible for their part in putting Jesus to death.
- God’s plan was accomplished. Yet those who acted wickedly were not excused from responsibility.
- Think about that. Peter spoke about God’s plan, but he still called people to repent.

**Acts 2:36-39**
> <sup>36</sup> “Therefore let all Israel be assured of this: God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Messiah.”
>
> <sup>37</sup> When the people heard this, they were cut to the heart and said to Peter and the other apostles, “Brothers, what shall we do?”
>
> <sup>38</sup> Peter replied, “Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins. And you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. <sup>39</sup> The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off—for all whom the Lord our God will call.”

- **God’s sovereignty is real, and human responsibility is real.**
- Christians differ in how they explain the relationship between these truths. But we must take both seriously.
- God can accomplish His purposes even when people act with evil intentions.
- That does not make evil good, and it does not erase accountability.

---

## Point 4: Does Repentance Matter?

- Can we change our minds? Does repentance matter? Do our prayers make a difference?
- Consider Nineveh in **Jonah 3**. God warned the city of coming judgment.
- The people believed Him. They humbled themselves and turned from their evil ways.

**Jonah 3:10**
> “When God saw what they did and how they turned from their evil ways, he relented and did not bring on them the destruction he had threatened.”

- God’s warning was meaningful. The people responded, and the judgment did not fall on them as announced.
- This does not mean God discovered something He had not known.
- It shows that God’s warnings, human repentance, and His response are all meaningful parts of how He deals with people.
- **2 Peter 3:9** tells us that the Lord is patient, “not wanting anyone to perish, but everyone to come to repentance.”
- God calls people to turn from sin and come to Him.
- We are not commanded to figure out everything God knows about tomorrow. **We are commanded to respond faithfully today.**
- We should never conclude that God’s foreknowledge makes repentance unnecessary or our prayers meaningless.

---

## Point 5: Why Should We Try to Do What Is Right?

- Someone might ask: “If God already knows everything I am going to do, why should I even try?”
- “If my future is already known, what difference do my decisions make?”
- Jesus answers with a call to action in **Mark 1:15**: **“Repent and believe the good news!”**
- **Matthew 16:24** says that anyone who wants to be Jesus’ disciple must deny themselves, take up their cross, and follow Him.
- Jesus did not tell people to wait until they understood God’s foreknowledge. He called them to repent, believe, and follow.
- We cannot excuse our sins by saying God already knew we would commit them.
- We cannot claim our choices do not matter when we:
  * refuse to forgive someone,
  * neglect prayer,
  * ignore God’s Word,
  * or turn away from someone in need.

**Galatians 6:7**
> “Do not be deceived: God cannot be mocked. A man reaps what he sows.”

- Our actions have consequences.
- Our decisions affect our character, our relationships, our witness, and the people around us.
- That does not mean we earn salvation through good works.
- **Ephesians 2:8-9** teaches that we are saved by grace through faith, not by works. Salvation is God’s gift through Jesus Christ.
- But the way we live still matters.
- Instead of asking: “Why should I try if God already knows my future?”
- We should ask: **“Since God knows me completely, how should I live before Him?”**
- The answer is clear: with faith, humility, repentance, love, and obedience.

---

## Point 6: God Invites You to Choose Him

- God’s knowledge of our future is a deep subject. But Scripture makes His invitation clear.

**Revelation 22:17**
> “Let the one who is thirsty come; and let the one who wishes take the free gift of the water of life.”

- The invitation is to come to Christ.
- **Ephesians 2:8-9** reminds us that salvation is by grace through faith.
- We cannot earn it, purchase it, or boast that we deserve it.
- God is the source of salvation, and Jesus Christ is our Savior.

**John 3:16**
> “For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life.”

- God showed His love by giving His Son for us. The Gospel calls us to put our faith in Jesus Christ.
- Christians differ in how they understand God’s sovereign will, His grace, and the human response to the Gospel.
- We should acknowledge those differences honestly. But the invitation of Scripture remains: **repent, believe, and follow Jesus.**
- Do not let questions about free will distract you from the One who gave His life to save you.
- The most important question is not whether you can explain every detail of God’s foreknowledge.
- **It is whether you are trusting Jesus Christ with your life.**

---

## Point 7: Living with the Mystery

- Let us return to our original question: If God already knows your future, do you really have free will?
- The Bible clearly teaches that God knows the end from the beginning. He knows our thoughts, words, and ways. Nothing surprises Him.
- The Bible also teaches that God is sovereign, and that human beings are accountable for their actions.
- Christians have debated for centuries how these truths fit together in every detail.
- We should not pretend that one illustration settles the entire mystery.
- But Scripture gives us a clear responsibility. We are called to:
  * repent of our sins,
  * believe in Jesus Christ,
  * love our neighbors,
  * forgive those who wrong us,
  * obey God’s Word,
  * and walk in faith.
- God’s foreknowledge is not permission to live carelessly.
- It is not an excuse to ignore His commands. And it is not a reason to believe your life has no purpose.
- **Your responsibility is not to understand everything God knows about tomorrow. Your responsibility is to trust Him and obey Him today.**
- Think about the decisions you are facing right now.
  * Is there a sin you need to turn away from?
  * Is there someone you need to forgive?
  * Is God calling you to take a step of faith that you have been avoiding?
- Do not wait until you understand every mystery of theology before you obey God’s Word.
- You may not know what tomorrow holds, but **you know the One who holds tomorrow in His Hand.**

**Proverbs 3:5-6**
> <sup>5</sup> Trust in the LORD with all your heart<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;and lean not on your own understanding;<br>
> <sup>6</sup> in all your ways submit to him,<br>
> &emsp;&emsp;and he will make your paths straight.

- That is how we live with the mystery of God’s foreknowledge.
- We trust Him, submit to Him, and follow His Word.
- We leave what we cannot fully understand in His hands. And we stay faithful in what He has clearly commanded us to do.

---

## Conclusion: What Will You Choose?

- Perhaps you have wondered whether your mistakes were inevitable, whether your prayers make a difference, or whether your decisions truly matter.
- Remember this: God knows your weaknesses, struggles, failures, and fears. He knows your heart.
- And the Gospel tells us that Jesus Christ came to save sinners.
  * You do not have to solve every mystery about God’s foreknowledge before you come to Him.
  * You do not have to understand every theological argument before you repent.
  * You do not have to see the entire road ahead before taking your next faithful step.
- **Today is the day** to respond to God’s Word. Today is the day to seek Him. Today is the day to put your faith in Jesus Christ.
  * If you have been living in sin, repent and turn to Him.
  * If you have been running from God, come home.
  * If you have been trusting in your own understanding, surrender your life to Jesus.
- And if you are already walking with the Lord, keep trusting Him, keep obeying Him, and keep loving Him with all your heart.
- I want to leave you with the words of **Joshua 24:15**: **“But as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD.”**
- God knows your future, but you must decide how you will respond to His call today.
  * Will you keep living for yourself, or will you surrender your life to Jesus Christ?
  * Will you keep holding on to sin, or will you ask God for the courage to turn away from it?
  * Will you trust only in your own understanding, or will you put your faith in the Lord?
- Put on the full armor of God (**Ephesians 6**). Stand firm in His truth.
- Ask Him for the strength to resist temptation and the courage to live a life that honors Him.
- We make choices every single day. Let us make those choices in a way that honors God.
- Give thanks to Him in all circumstances, trust His promises, and stay faithful to His Word.
- We may not fully understand how God’s perfect foreknowledge relates to our decisions.
- But we can know this: God is faithful, His Word is true, and His invitation to come to Christ is real.
- So I leave you with one final question: **If God already knows your future, will you trust Him with your present?**
- Choose to seek Him. Choose to follow His Word.
- Choose to live for Jesus Christ. Not because you understand every mystery, but because you trust the One who holds your future.
- May God give you the courage to turn away from sin, the faith to trust His promises, and the strength to walk in obedience every day.

**Your decisions matter. You must choose.**
> *“Now choose life, so that you and your children may live.”* — Deuteronomy 30:19

God Bless you all in The Mighty Name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth.
